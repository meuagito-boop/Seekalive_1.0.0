# Source evidence: Engagement, user satisfaction, and the amplification of divisive content on social media

- Source URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11894805/
- Journal DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf062
- Access date: 2026-10-26
- Published: 2025-03-05, PNAS Nexus 4(3), pgaf062
- Source type: Peer-reviewed primary research article; open-access full text at PubMed Central

## Evidence captured from the article abstract

The article describes a preregistered algorithmic audit. Compared with a reverse-chronological baseline, Twitter/X’s engagement-based ranking algorithm amplified emotionally charged, out-group-hostile content that users reported made them feel worse about their political out-group. The authors also report that users did not prefer the political tweets selected by the algorithm according to their stated preferences. An alternative ranking based on stated preferences reduced angry, partisan, and out-group-hostile content, while potentially reinforcing pro-attitudinal content. The authors conclude that integrating stated preferences could improve online discourse, but identify potential trade-offs requiring further research.

## Method and limitations

This is an audit/comparative evaluation of ranked, chronological, and preference-based selections, not a randomized longitudinal deployment of three feed products. It concerns political tweets and measured content/preferences, so it does not establish effects on general social-feed retention, user satisfaction overall, or Seekalive’s audiences. It supports treating expressed user preference as a potentially distinct ranking signal, not as proof that a preference control will improve all outcomes.
