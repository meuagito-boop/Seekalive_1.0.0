# Source evidence: The political effects of X’s feed algorithm

- Source URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10098-2
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10098-2
- Access date: 2026-10-26
- Published: 2026-02-18, Nature 652, 416–423
- Source type: Peer-reviewed primary research article

## Evidence captured from the article abstract

The authors report a 2023 field experiment on X. Active US-based users were randomly assigned to an algorithmic or chronological feed for seven weeks, while political attitudes and online behavior were measured. Among users initially assigned to chronological feeds, seven weeks on the algorithmic feed increased engagement and shifted some political opinions and account-following behavior in a more conservative direction relative to users who stayed on chronological feeds. The article reports no comparable effects when switching in the opposite direction, from algorithmic to chronological. Neither switching the algorithm on nor off significantly changed affective polarization or self-reported partisanship.

## Interpretation and limitations evident from the source

- Random assignment supports causal interpretation for the studied treatment, participating active US X users, outcomes, and 2023 platform context; it does not establish effects for other platforms or a new product.
- The comparison is a particular proprietary X algorithm against chronological ordering, not a generic test of all ranked feeds.
- The asymmetric result cautions against assuming that switching to chronological ordering immediately reverses effects of prior algorithmic exposure.
- The abstract does not provide sample size or enough detail to quantify the size of effects; consult full article methods/results for those details.
