# Search evidence: feed-ranking gap fill

- Search access date: 2026-10-26
- Search method: Direct HTML requests to Brave Search (`https://search.brave.com/search`) after official search callbacks were unavailable in the parent environment.
- Purpose: discover empirical research; the evidence below records search leads, not proof beyond the linked sources.

## Focused queries and leads

1. Query: “randomized chronological algorithmic social media feed Facebook Instagram 2023 experiment”
   - Lead: Science, “How do social media feed algorithms affect attitudes and behavior in an election campaign?” https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abp9364
   - Search result described a 2023 randomized intervention moving Facebook and Instagram users from default ranking to reverse chronological / following-only feeds; result text noted changes to on-platform experience and content exposure, but no significant changes to several political attitudes over three months.
   - Lead: UNC institutional repository copy/search record: https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/wp9891401

2. Query: “2023 Facebook Instagram feed ranking experiments chronological political exposure diversity engagement”
   - Lead: same Science paper, DOI above. Search result surfaced an author correction notice concerning variables measuring exposure to and engagement with untrustworthy sources on Instagram. This is a reason to consult the corrected record and avoid relying on those specific Instagram measures without checking the correction.

3. Query: “peer reviewed user control social media feed ranking chronological algorithm 2024 2025 study”
   - Lead: Milli et al., PNAS Nexus (2025), “Engagement, user satisfaction, and the amplification of divisive content on social media,” https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf062
   - Search results characterize this as an audit comparing engagement-based ranking with stated user preferences; full-text evidence is saved in `gapfill-feed-ranking-02-pnas-stated-preferences-audit.md`.

4. Query: “effects of feed ranking on exposure diversity engagement social media experimental evidence”
   - Lead: Gauthier et al., Nature (2026), “The political effects of X’s feed algorithm,” https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10098-2
   - Search result described a 7-week randomized feed experiment on X, reporting increased engagement and some conservative shifts among users switched from chronological to algorithmic feeds. Full article abstract evidence is saved in `gapfill-feed-ranking-01-nature-x-field-experiment.md`.
   - Additional lead: “Reranking partisan animosity in algorithmic social media feeds alters affective polarization,” Science DOI https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adu5584 (search lead only; not used as a factual basis here).
